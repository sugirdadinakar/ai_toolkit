# Research Synthesis

Turn a mess of raw user signal into themes, insights, and decisions.

## When to use
- The user has interview notes, survey data, support tickets, reviews, or call transcripts to analyze.
- They need to find patterns across many data points and rank what matters.
- They want research to feed a prioritization or roadmap decision.

## Process

1. **Intake.** Confirm the sources and the question the research is meant to answer. If no guiding question exists, help define one — synthesis without a question produces trivia.

2. **Code the data.** Tag each observation with:
   - the **theme** it belongs to,
   - the **job-to-be-done** or pain it reflects,
   - the **sentiment** (blocker / friction / delight / neutral),
   - the **source** (so claims stay traceable).

3. **Cluster into themes.** Group observations. For each theme capture:
   - a one-line insight statement (what we learned, not what we saw),
   - **frequency** (how many sources raised it),
   - **impact** (how much it affects the user's job),
   - representative **verbatim quotes** (keep the user's words),
   - **confidence** (strong / tentative — note small samples).

4. **Rank.** Order themes by frequency × impact. Separate what's validated from what's a hypothesis needing more evidence.

5. **Recommend.** Translate the top themes into concrete next steps: what to build, what to investigate further, what to ignore. Tie each to the originating evidence.

## Guardrails
- Distinguish what users **said** from what they **did** — behavior beats opinion.
- Watch for leading-question bias in the source material and flag it.
- Don't over-index on the loudest voice; weight by frequency and representativeness.
- Keep every insight traceable to its source so others can audit it.

## Output
A structured synthesis: guiding question, ranked themes (with frequency, impact, quotes, confidence), and prioritized recommendations. Offer to feed the top opportunities into a PRD or discovery plan.
